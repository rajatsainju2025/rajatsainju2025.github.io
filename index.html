Rajat Sainju

I'm an AI/ML scientist at the Advanced Photon Source at Argonne National Laboratory. I build production LLM systems, including agentic RAG and AI harnesses, that help run a large accelerator facility, and I develop machine learning for accelerators and materials science.

My postdoctoral advisors at Argonne are Dr. Michael Borland and Dr. Yine Sun. I did my Ph.D. in Materials Science and Engineering at UConn with Prof. Yuanyuan Zhu, where I developed deep learning tools, like DefectTrack, that analyze in-situ electron microscopy videos in real time. Before that, I studied Engineering Physics at Ramapo College.

Operando Elucidation of Filamentous Carbon Gasification Kinetics and Mechanisms on a Spent Ni/CeO₂ Catalyst

Monia R. Nielsen, Tianyu Li, Rajat Sainju, Seth March, Chunxiang Zhu, Pu-Xian Gao, Steven L. Suib, Yuanyuan Zhu
ACS Catalysis 2025
TLDRWatches a spent Ni/CeO₂ catalyst being regenerated in air inside an environmental TEM and, with semantic segmentation, separates three ways its carbon filaments burn off: catalytic, noncatalytic and cooperative. The last two turn out to be more common than expected, and catalytic gasification is limited by carbon diffusion through Ni(−Cₓ) rather than the usual bulk-diffusion model.

Operando Spatially and Time-Resolved Reduction of Hollow Polycrystalline NiO: How to Promote Ni Nanoparticle Redispersion

Rajat Sainju, Tianyu Li, Monia R. Nielsen, Yuanyuan Zhu
Nano Letters 2025
TLDRFollows hollow NiO grains being reduced in an environmental TEM and measures their kinetics with semantic segmentation. A milder 450 °C reduction slows and desynchronizes neighbouring grains, which breaks the oxide into more, smaller Ni particles: redispersion needs both a hollow oxide and slow, asynchronous reduction.
